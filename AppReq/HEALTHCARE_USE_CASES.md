# HEALTHCARE INDUSTRY INCIDENT MANAGEMENT USE CASES

## Document Overview
This document provides comprehensive, day-to-day incident management use cases specific to the healthcare industry. These use cases are based on 15+ years of healthcare operations experience and cover all major incident types encountered in hospital, clinic, and healthcare facility operations.

---

## USE CASE 1: MEDICATION ADMINISTRATION ERROR

### Description
A nurse or healthcare provider administers the wrong dose, wrong medication, wrong route, or medication to the wrong patient.

### Frequency
**Daily** (most common preventable adverse event in healthcare)

### Severity Range
**Low to Critical**
- **Low:** Medication given 30 minutes late, no patient harm
- **Medium:** Wrong dose administered, patient monitored, no adverse effects
- **High:** Wrong medication given, requires medical intervention
- **Critical:** Life-threatening allergic reaction, overdose, or death

### Key Data Points Required
- Patient identifier (anonymized/de-identified for HIPAA compliance)
- Medication name (generic and brand)
- Prescribed dose vs. administered dose
- Route (oral, IV, IM, subcutaneous, topical, etc.)
- Time of administration vs. scheduled time
- Prescriber name/ID
- Nurse/administrator ID
- Pharmacy involvement (if dispensing error contributed)
- Contributing factors (look-alike/sound-alike drugs, interruptions, fatigue, staffing)
- Patient outcome and harm level (NCC MERP Index)
- Corrective actions taken immediately
- Witnesses present
- Medication reconciliation status
- Barcode scanning compliance
- Double-check procedure followed (if applicable)

### Regulatory Requirements
- **Joint Commission** reporting (sentinel events if severe harm/death)
- **State Board of Nursing** notification (if required by state)
- **FDA MedWatch** reporting for serious adverse events
- **Internal Pharmacy & Therapeutics Committee** review
- **HIPAA-compliant** documentation (no PHI in open text fields)
- **NCC MERP** (National Coordinating Council for Medication Error Reporting) classification
- **ISMP** (Institute for Safe Medication Practices) voluntary reporting

### Stakeholders
- Nurse Manager
- Pharmacy Director
- Patient Safety Officer
- Risk Management
- Attending Physician
- Quality Improvement Committee
- Legal (if litigation risk)
- Patient/Family (disclosure per policy)
- Chief Nursing Officer (if severe)
- Medication Safety Committee

### Workflow Requirements

**Immediate Actions (0-1 hour):**
1. Immediate supervisor notification
2. Patient assessment and intervention
3. Physician notification
4. Medication error documented in patient chart
5. Incident report initiated

**Within 24 Hours:**
1. Comprehensive patient monitoring
2. Pharmacy review of medication orders
3. Initial investigation by nurse manager
4. Family notification if harm occurred

**Within 72 Hours:**
1. Root cause analysis initiated for high/critical events
2. Peer review committee assessment
3. Education/remediation plan if indicated
4. System-level corrective action identification
5. Similar medication orders reviewed facility-wide

**Approval Requirements:**
- Pharmacy Director approval for medication system changes
- Patient Safety Officer sign-off on investigation
- Medical Executive Committee review for severe events
- Board of Directors notification for sentinel events

## USE CASE 2: PATIENT FALL WITH INJURY

### Description
Patient falls while in hospital/facility care, resulting in injury or potential injury. Includes falls from bed, during toileting, while ambulating, or during transfers.

### Frequency
**Weekly to Daily** (depending on facility size and patient population)

### Severity Range
**Low to Critical**
- **Low:** Fall without injury, patient assisted up immediately
- **Medium:** Minor injury (bruise, abrasion, minor laceration)
- **High:** Fracture, laceration requiring sutures, head injury
- **Critical:** Hip fracture, intracranial hemorrhage, spinal injury, death

### Key Data Points Required
- Patient fall risk score prior to event (Morse, Humpty Dumpty, etc.)
- Time and location of fall
- Witnessed vs. unwitnessed
- Activity at time of fall (toileting, transferring, ambulating, reaching)
- Environmental factors (wet floor, lighting, clutter, bed height)
- Assistive devices in use (walker, cane, bed alarm, chair alarm)
- Medications contributing to fall risk (sedatives, BP meds, diuretics)
- Injury type and severity
- Post-fall assessment findings (vital signs, neuro checks)
- X-rays/imaging results
- Staffing levels at time of incident
- Call light accessibility and response time
- Patient cognitive status
- Prior falls during admission
- Footwear worn by patient

### Regulatory Requirements
- **Joint Commission** review and analysis
- **CMS** quality measure reporting (publicly reported)
- **State Department of Health** notification (if serious injury/death)
- **National Database of Nursing Quality Indicators (NDNQI)** reporting
- **Hospital Compare** public reporting
- Potential **survey tag** if pattern identified during inspection

### Stakeholders
- Nursing leadership (Unit Manager, Charge Nurse)
- Physical therapy/Occupational therapy
- Risk management
- Patient safety committee
- Facilities/Environmental services
- Physician/Provider
- Patient/Family
- Legal counsel (if serious injury)
- Quality improvement department
- Fall prevention committee

### Workflow Requirements

**Immediate Actions (0-1 hour):**
1. Patient assessment and treatment
2. Physician notification
3. Injury documentation
4. X-rays/imaging if indicated
5. Incident report initiated
6. Family notification

**Within 24 Hours:**
1. 24-hour post-fall monitoring protocol
2. Fall risk reassessment
3. Interventions implemented (bed alarm, 1:1 sitter, low bed, etc.)
4. Environmental assessment of fall location
5. Supervisor investigation completed

**Within 48-72 Hours:**
1. Multidisciplinary fall prevention team review
2. Root cause analysis for injurious falls
3. Trend analysis for unit/facility
4. Staff education if practice gaps identified
5. Equipment assessment (bed alarms, mats, etc.)

**Approval Requirements:**
- Nursing leadership approval of intervention plan
- Patient Safety Committee review for patterns
- Risk Management sign-off for serious injuries

## USE CASE 3: WRONG SITE/WRONG PROCEDURE/WRONG PATIENT SURGERY

### Description
Surgical procedure performed on wrong body site (e.g., wrong knee), wrong procedure performed, or surgery performed on wrong patient. This is a "Never Event" - should never occur.

### Frequency
**Rare** (but catastrophic when it occurs)

### Severity Range
**Critical Only**
- All instances are considered **Critical/Sentinel Events** regardless of patient outcome

### Key Data Points Required
- Scheduled procedure vs. performed procedure
- Correct patient verification process followed (at each stage)
- Surgical site marking documentation and photos
- Time-out checklist completion and signatures
- Team members present (surgeon, anesthesia, nursing, tech)
- How error was discovered (during procedure vs. post-op)
- Contributing factors (communication breakdown, rushing, assumption, similar patient names)
- Patient consent form review
- Surgical schedule accuracy
- Pre-op verification compliance
- Images/documentation of incorrect site marking
- Patient outcome and additional procedures required
- All communication transcripts

### Regulatory Requirements
- **MANDATORY Joint Commission Sentinel Event reporting** (within 15 days)
- **State Department of Health immediate notification** (within 24 hours typically)
- **CMS reporting** as Never Event/Hospital-Acquired Condition
- **National Practitioner Data Bank** reporting (potentially)
- **Malpractice insurance notification** (immediate)
- **Board of Medicine** review (potentially for provider)
- **Serious Reportable Event (SRE)** - Never Event
- **NQF (National Quality Forum)** serious reportable event

### Stakeholders
- Chief Medical Officer
- Chief Nursing Officer
- Surgery department chair
- Risk management (immediate involvement)
- Legal counsel (immediate involvement)
- Patient safety officer
- Anesthesia department head
- All OR team members involved
- Patient/Family (full disclosure required within 24 hours)
- Hospital administration/CEO
- Board of directors
- State licensing board
- Quality and safety committee

### Workflow Requirements

**Immediate Actions (0-2 hours):**
1. **STOP procedure immediately** if discovered intra-operatively
2. Immediate leadership notification (within 1 hour)
3. Patient care and safety prioritized
4. Equipment/documentation preservation
5. Team debriefing (separate from investigation)

**Within 24 Hours:**
1. Patient/family disclosure with full transparency
2. Comprehensive incident report completed
3. All team members interviewed separately
4. Medical record review
5. Leadership notification to Board and regulatory bodies

**Within 45 Days:**
1. Comprehensive **Root Cause Analysis (RCA)** completed
2. Action plan developed with specific interventions
3. External expert review may be required
4. System-wide protocol review
5. Staff retraining on Universal Protocol

**Within 90 Days:**
1. Ongoing monitoring and auditing of Universal Protocol compliance
2. Action plan implementation verification
3. Culture of safety assessment
4. Policy and procedure updates

**Approval Requirements:**
- CEO/CNO/CMO approval of RCA findings
- Board of Directors notification and approval of action plan
- Regulatory submission approval
- External expert review (may be mandated)

## USE CASE 4: HEALTHCARE-ASSOCIATED INFECTION (HAI) - CLABSI

### Description
Patient develops **Central Line-Associated Bloodstream Infection (CLABSI)** - bloodstream infection related to central venous catheter. One of the most tracked HAIs.

### Frequency
**Weekly to Monthly** (varies by facility size and prevention efforts)

### Severity Range
**Medium to Critical**
- **Medium:** Infection identified early, responds to antibiotics, no complications
- **High:** Sepsis develops, ICU transfer required, extended hospital stay
- **Critical:** Septic shock, multi-organ failure, death

### Key Data Points Required
- Central line insertion date and time
- Central line location (internal jugular, subclavian, femoral, PICC)
- Type of central line (triple lumen, PICC, Hickman, port)
- Insertion site care documentation (dressing changes, cleaning solution)
- Blood culture results (organism, antibiotic sensitivity)
- Clinical symptoms onset date/time
- Days from insertion to infection (line days calculation)
- Compliance with insertion bundle (documented checklist)
- Compliance with maintenance bundle (daily review)
- Hand hygiene observations
- Healthcare worker involved in insertion (certification status)
- ICU vs. non-ICU location
- Device necessity daily review
- Chlorhexidine bath compliance (if applicable)

### Regulatory Requirements
- **CDC National Healthcare Safety Network (NHSN)** reporting (mandatory)
- **CMS Hospital-Acquired Condition (HAC)** - payment reduction penalty
- **State mandatory reporting** (most states require HAI reporting)
- **Joint Commission** infection control standards
- **Publicly reported data** (Hospital Compare, Leapfrog)
- **Quality improvement initiative** - Hospital Quality Incentive

### Stakeholders
- Infection preventionist/epidemiologist
- Infectious disease physician (consultant)
- Primary attending physician
- Nursing leadership (Unit Manager, CNO)
- Quality department
- Pharmacy (antimicrobial stewardship team)
- Patient safety committee
- Hospital epidemiologist
- Risk management
- ICU Medical Director

### Workflow Requirements

**Immediate Actions (0-24 hours):**
1. Infection prevention team investigation within 24 hours
2. Chart review for bundle compliance (insertion and maintenance)
3. Line removal (usually required unless no alternative access)
4. Antibiotic therapy initiation per protocol
5. Blood cultures obtained before antibiotics

**Within 48-72 Hours:**
1. Root cause analysis for prevention opportunities
2. Compliance audit of similar patients with central lines
3. Unit-based education on bundle elements
4. Review of hand hygiene compliance data
5. Assessment of environmental cleaning

**Within 30 Days:**
1. NHSN reporting completed
2. Compliance monitoring intensification
3. Monthly HAI committee review
4. Public reporting compliance
5. Action plan for prevention implemented

**Approval Requirements:**
- Infection Prevention Director sign-off on investigation
- Quality Committee review
- Medical Executive Committee notification if trending increase
- State reporting submission approval

## USE CASE 5: HOSPITAL-ACQUIRED PRESSURE INJURY (HAPI) - Stage 3 or 4

### Description
Patient develops **stage 3 or 4 pressure injury** (pressure ulcer/bedsore) while in hospital care, indicating full-thickness tissue loss.

### Frequency
**Weekly** (varies by facility and patient acuity)

### Severity Range
**Medium to High**
- **Medium:** Stage 3 pressure injury, manageable with advanced wound care
- **High:** Stage 4 or unstageable deep tissue injury, requires extensive treatment, potentially surgical debridement

### Key Data Points Required
- Admission skin assessment (baseline - documented as present on admission or not)
- Braden Scale score (risk assessment tool)
- Location of pressure injury (sacrum, heel, trochanter, etc.)
- Stage of pressure injury (Stage 3, Stage 4, Unstageable, Deep Tissue Injury)
- Suspected vs. confirmed hospital-acquired
- Turning/repositioning schedule compliance documentation
- Support surface in use (specialty bed, low air loss mattress, alternating pressure)
- Nutrition assessment (albumin, pre-albumin, dietary intake)
- Moisture management (incontinence care)
- Photos of wound (date-stamped, with measurement tool)
- Days to development (if hospital-acquired)
- Co-morbidities (diabetes, vascular disease, immobility)
- Friction/shear risk factors

### Regulatory Requirements
- **CMS Hospital-Acquired Condition (HAC)** - no payment for stage 3/4 acquired in hospital
- **State reporting** (varies by state - some require HAPI reporting)
- **Joint Commission** review as quality indicator
- **Publicly reported quality measure** (Hospital Compare)
- **Survey citation risk** during state inspections
- **Staging validation** (must be accurate for reporting)

### Stakeholders
- Wound care nurse specialist/WOCN (Wound, Ostomy, Continence Nurse)
- Nursing leadership (Unit Manager, CNO)
- Wound care physician/medical director
- Dietitian/Nutritional services
- Physical therapy
- Risk management
- Quality improvement
- Patient/Family
- Wound care committee
- Skin care team

### Workflow Requirements

**Immediate Actions (0-24 hours):**
1. Immediate wound care consultation (WOCN)
2. Pressure redistribution intervention (specialty surface)
3. Nutritional assessment and intervention
4. Photo documentation (wound measurement)
5. Physician notification and wound care orders

**Within 48-72 Hours:**
1. Root cause analysis for hospital-acquired injuries
2. Prevention bundle reinforcement for patient
3. Multidisciplinary wound care plan
4. Review of repositioning compliance
5. Assessment of support surface adequacy

**Ongoing:**
1. Daily interdisciplinary wound rounds
2. Weekly wound measurements and photos
3. Tracking and trending for unit/facility
4. Monthly pressure injury committee review
5. Staff education on prevention

**Approval Requirements:**
- Wound Care Committee review
- Quality improvement sign-off on preventability assessment
- Risk Management notification for stage 4 injuries
- Staging validation by certified wound care nurse

## USE CASE 6: PATIENT ELOPEMENT/UNAUTHORIZED DISCHARGE

### Description
Patient leaves facility without medical clearance or proper discharge, posing safety risk to themselves. Includes competent adults leaving against medical advice (AMA) without proper documentation and incompetent/at-risk patients who wander or escape.

### Frequency
**Monthly to Weekly** (varies by facility type and patient population)

### Severity Range
**Medium to Critical**
- **Medium:** Competent patient leaves AMA, located quickly, returns safely
- **High:** At-risk patient (dementia, psych hold) missing for extended period
- **Critical:** Patient harmed while away from facility, death, suicide

### Key Data Points Required
- Patient mental status/cognitive function assessment
- Legal status (involuntary psychiatric hold, competent adult, incompetent, pediatric)
- Last known location and time seen
- Clothing description and belongings taken
- Medications patient is missing (especially critical medications)
- Medical conditions requiring ongoing treatment
- Exit point identified (door alarm breach, secured unit, visitor exit)
- Staff awareness of elopement risk score
- Elopement precautions in place (1:1, locked unit, alarm, etc.)
- Time patient found/returned
- Patient condition upon return
- Law enforcement involvement
- Search efforts documented
- Surveillance camera review

### Regulatory Requirements
- **Law enforcement notification** (immediate if incompetent/psych hold/pediatric)
- **State mental health authority** (if psychiatric involuntary hold)
- **CMS reporting** (if results in patient safety event/harm)
- **Joint Commission** review (patient rights vs. safety)
- **State licensing board** review (potential citation)
- **AMA documentation** (if competent patient - specific forms required)

### Stakeholders
- Nursing supervisor (House Supervisor)
- Security department
- Facility administrator/Administrator on Call
- Risk management (immediate notification)
- Attending physician
- Patient safety officer
- Local law enforcement (if appropriate)
- Family/Legal guardian (immediate notification)
- Legal counsel (if involuntary patient or high risk)
- Psychiatric consultation (if applicable)
- Social services

### Workflow Requirements

**Immediate Actions (0-30 minutes):**
1. **Immediate search of facility** (all floors, departments, grounds)
2. **Security notification and involvement**
3. **Supervisor notification** (House Supervisor/Administrator on Call)
4. **Law enforcement notification** per protocol (immediate if at-risk patient)
5. **Family/guardian notification**

**Within 1-2 Hours:**
1. Physician notification
2. Incident command activation if prolonged search
3. Surveillance camera review
4. Search radius expansion (neighboring areas, transportation hubs)
5. Patient description dissemination

**Upon Patient Return:**
1. Patient assessment (physical and mental health evaluation)
2. Psychiatric evaluation if applicable
3. Medical clearance
4. Enhanced monitoring implementation (1:1 observation, alarm, relocation)
5. Family meeting

**Within 24-48 Hours:**
1. Comprehensive incident investigation
2. Security system review (door alarms, locks, access points)
3. Elopement risk reassessment
4. Policy and procedure review
5. Staff education if gaps identified

**Approval Requirements:**
- Risk Management sign-off on investigation
- Administration approval of security enhancements
- Medical Staff leadership review if clinical factors
- Legal review if involuntary hold or competency questions

## USE CASE 7: MEDICATION DISPENSING ERROR (PHARMACY)

### Description
Pharmacy dispenses wrong medication, wrong strength, wrong quantity, mislabeled medication, or medication to wrong patient. Error occurs in the pharmacy before medication reaches nursing unit.

### Frequency
**Weekly** (detected errors; many caught by verification before reaching patient)

### Severity Range
**Low to Critical**
- **Low:** Error caught by pharmacist verification before leaving pharmacy
- **Medium:** Error caught by nurse before administration to patient
- **High:** Patient receives wrong medication, requires intervention
- **Critical:** Life-threatening reaction, overdose, death

### Key Data Points Required
- Medication ordered vs. medication dispensed
- Prescriber and order details
- Pharmacist who dispensed
- Pharmacy technician involved (if applicable)
- Verification steps completed (first check, second check)
- Contributing factors (similar packaging, sound-alike drugs, LASA drugs, interruptions, workload)
- How error was discovered (pharmacist, nurse, patient, automated system)
- Patient received medication (yes/no)
- Patient outcome if medication administered
- System safeguards in place (barcode, automated dispensing, clinical decision support)
- Time of day and staffing levels
- Distractions or interruptions noted

### Regulatory Requirements
- **State Board of Pharmacy reporting** (varies by state - serious errors may require notification)
- **FDA MedWatch** (if serious patient outcome)
- **Joint Commission medication management standards**
- **ISMP (Institute for Safe Medication Practices) voluntary reporting**
- **USP <800>** (if hazardous drug involved)
- **Internal quality assurance review**

### Stakeholders
- Pharmacy Director
- Pharmacist involved
- Pharmacy technician involved
- Patient safety officer
- Pharmacy & Therapeutics Committee
- Risk management
- Physician/Prescriber
- Nursing (if medication reached floor)
- Medication Safety Committee
- Quality improvement

### Workflow Requirements

**Immediate Actions (0-2 hours):**
1. Immediate retrieval of incorrect medication (if left pharmacy)
2. Patient assessment if medication was administered
3. Physician notification
4. Correct medication dispensed and verified
5. Incident documentation

**Within 24-48 Hours:**
1. Pharmacist peer review
2. Contributing factors analysis
3. System improvement identification
4. Technology review (barcode scanning, CPOE integration, alerts)
5. Workflow assessment

**Within 1 Week:**
1. Root cause analysis for serious errors
2. Education/remediation for involved staff
3. Similar medication review (LASA separation, storage)
4. Monthly pharmacy safety committee review
5. Trending analysis

**Approval Requirements:**
- Pharmacy Director approval of investigation findings
- Medication Safety Committee review
- Quality improvement sign-off on system changes
- P&T Committee notification for formulary implications

## USE CASE 8: SURGICAL RETAINED FOREIGN OBJECT

### Description
Surgical sponge, instrument, needle, or other item unintentionally left inside patient's body after surgical procedure. This is a "Never Event."

### Frequency
**Rare** (estimated 1 in 1,000-1,500 operations, but preventable)

### Severity Range
**High to Critical**
- **High:** Object discovered on routine post-op imaging, reoperation required for removal, no infection
- **Critical:** Sepsis, organ perforation/damage, delayed discovery, severe infection, death

### Key Data Points Required
- Type of object retained (surgical sponge, needle, instrument, guidewire, catheter fragment)
- Surgical procedure performed
- Surgical count documentation (initial count, interim counts, final count) - all documented and signed
- Count discrepancies noted intraoperatively (and actions taken)
- X-ray taken if count incorrect (per policy)
- Discovery method (routine post-op imaging, reoperation, patient symptoms, wound exploration)
- Time from initial surgery to discovery
- Surgical team members (surgeon, assistant, scrub tech, circulating nurse, anesthesia)
- Patient outcome (reoperation, infection, organ damage)
- Reoperation details
- Patient notification timeline
- All count sheets and documentation

### Regulatory Requirements
- **MANDATORY Joint Commission Sentinel Event reporting** (within 15 days)
- **State Department of Health notification** (immediate to 24 hours, varies by state)
- **CMS serious reportable event** (Never Event)
- **National Practitioner Data Bank** reporting (potentially for involved providers)
- **Malpractice carrier notification** (immediate)
- **Board of Medicine review** (potentially for surgeon)
- **NQF Serious Reportable Event**
- **Public disclosure** (varies by state transparency laws)

### Stakeholders
- Chief of Surgery
- Operating room director/manager
- Patient safety officer
- Risk management (immediate involvement)
- Legal counsel (immediate involvement)
- All surgical team members (surgeon, assistants, nurses, techs)
- Patient/Family
- Hospital leadership (CEO, CMO, CNO)
- Quality and safety committee
- Medical executive committee
- Board of directors
- Anesthesia department
- Radiology (for imaging)

### Workflow Requirements

**Immediate Actions (0-4 hours):**
1. **Immediate patient imaging** (X-ray, CT) to locate object
2. **Patient stabilization** and medical intervention
3. **Surgical consultation** for removal (urgent/emergent reoperation)
4. **Leadership notification within 24 hours** (Risk, Administration, Patient Safety)
5. **Equipment and count sheets preservation**
6. **Team members identified and notified** of investigation

**Within 24-48 Hours:**
1. **Full disclosure to patient/family** (within 24 hours typically)
2. **Removal procedure planned/performed**
3. **Initial investigation** - all count documentation reviewed
4. **Regulatory notifications** completed
5. **Legal counsel consultation**

**Within 45 Days:**
1. **Comprehensive Root Cause Analysis (RCA)** (required by Joint Commission)
2. **External expert review** (may be mandated)
3. **Surgical team debriefing** (separate from investigation)
4. **Count policy review and enhancement**
5. **Technology solutions evaluation** (RFID sponges, x-ray detectable items)

**Within 90 Days:**
1. **Action plan implementation**
2. **Staff retraining on count procedures**
3. **Policy updates and communication**
4. **Auditing of compliance** with count procedures
5. **Board reporting and oversight**

**Approval Requirements:**
- CEO/CMO/CNO approval of RCA and action plan
- Board of Directors notification
- Regulatory submission approval
- External review (if mandated by state/accreditor)
- Medical Executive Committee review and approval

## USE CASE 9: BLOOD TRANSFUSION REACTION

### Description
Patient experiences adverse reaction to blood product transfusion, ranging from mild allergic reaction to life-threatening complications.

### Frequency
**Weekly** (minor reactions) to **Monthly** (serious reactions)

### Severity Range
**Low to Critical**
- **Low:** Mild allergic reaction (urticaria/hives), easily treated, transfusion continues or restarted
- **Medium:** Febrile non-hemolytic reaction, transfusion stopped, symptomatic treatment
- **High:** Acute hemolytic reaction (ABO incompatibility), respiratory distress, requires ICU
- **Critical:** Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI), anaphylaxis, DIC, death

### Key Data Points Required
- Blood product type (RBC, platelets, FFP, cryoprecipitate)
- Patient blood type and antibody screen results
- Blood product unit number(s)
- Compatibility testing results (crossmatch)
- Verification process documentation (two-person bedside check per policy)
- Vital signs before transfusion, during, and after reaction
- Reaction type and symptoms (fever, chills, hives, SOB, hypotension, chest pain)
- Time from transfusion start to reaction onset
- Volume transfused before stopping
- Interventions provided (medications, oxygen, fluids)
- Blood bank notification time
- Product quarantine and return to blood bank
- Lab work ordered (hemolysis panel, culture of product, repeat type and screen, DAT, haptoglobin, LDH, bilirubin)
- Medications given with transfusion
- Prior transfusion reactions

### Regulatory Requirements
- **FDA reporting** (serious reactions causing death, life-threatening illness, hospitalization, disability - within 24 hours)
- **Blood bank regulatory compliance** (AABB standards, FDA, state regulations)
- **State health department notification** (for serious reactions per state requirements)
- **Joint Commission** standards for safe blood administration
- **Hospital transfusion committee review** (all reactions reviewed)
- **Hemovigilance reporting** (if facility participates)

### Stakeholders
- Blood bank director/pathologist
- Transfusion safety officer
- Ordering physician
- Bedside nurse
- Patient safety officer
- Risk management
- Pharmacy (if medications contributed)
- Critical care team (if severe reaction)
- Pulmonology (if TRALI)
- Transfusion committee

### Workflow Requirements

**Immediate Actions (0-30 minutes):**
1. **Immediate cessation of transfusion** (stop infusion, keep line open with saline)
2. **Patient stabilization** (oxygen, medications, vital signs monitoring)
3. **Blood bank notification** immediately
4. **Product return to blood bank** (with tubing attached, keep refrigerated)
5. **Physician notification**
6. **Repeat blood type and antibody screen** if hemolytic reaction suspected

**Within 1-2 Hours:**
1. Investigation of clerical errors (bedside check, labeling, patient ID)
2. Lab work ordered and monitored
3. Second blood product from different donor (if transfusion still needed)
4. Detailed documentation in patient chart and transfusion reaction form

**Within 24-48 Hours:**
1. Blood bank investigation completed
2. Root cause analysis for serious reactions
3. Transfusion reaction committee review scheduled
4. FDA reporting if applicable
5. Patient monitoring for delayed hemolytic reactions

**Within 30 Days:**
1. Transfusion reaction committee formal review
2. Hemovigilance reporting (if participating)
3. Process improvement identified
4. Education if procedure gaps identified
5. Monthly trending analysis

**Approval Requirements:**
- Blood Bank Director sign-off on investigation
- Transfusion Safety Officer approval
- Quality improvement review
- FDA submission approval if required

## USE CASE 10: DIAGNOSTIC ERROR - MISSED CRITICAL FINDING ON RADIOLOGY

### Description
Radiologist or provider misses critical finding on imaging study (X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound), resulting in delayed diagnosis and treatment.

### Frequency
**Monthly** (varies by imaging volume - estimated 3-5% error rate in radiology)

### Severity Range
**Medium to Critical**
- **Medium:** Delayed diagnosis by days to weeks, but no permanent harm to patient
- **High:** Significant delay in treatment (weeks to months), worse patient outcome or prognosis
- **Critical:** Missed cancer diagnosis, missed fracture with complications, missed PE/DVT leading to death

### Key Data Points Required
- Imaging modality (X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound, fluoroscopy, nuclear medicine)
- Body region imaged
- Preliminary read vs. final interpretation (if different radiologists)
- Critical finding that was missed (fracture, mass, pneumothorax, hemorrhage, etc.)
- When finding was eventually discovered (subsequent imaging, clinical deterioration, autopsy)
- Time delay in diagnosis (days/weeks/months)
- Patient outcome impact (treatment delay, disease progression, death)
- Radiologist credentials and experience
- Radiologist workload/volume at time of study
- System/technology factors (image quality, PACS issues, viewing conditions)
- Communication of results (critical results policy followed?)
- Clinical correlation and history provided with order
- Comparison studies available

### Regulatory Requirements
- **State medical board** notification (potentially - depends on severity and state)
- **Malpractice carrier notification**
- **Peer review protection** (varies by state - HCQIA protections)
- **Joint Commission patient safety standards**
- **ACR (American College of Radiology) guidelines** and peer review standards
- **Disclosure to patient** (if significant impact)

### Stakeholders
- Radiology department chair
- Radiologist who read study (initial and final if different)
- Ordering physician/provider
- Patient safety officer
- Risk management (immediate if serious)
- Legal counsel (if serious)
- Quality improvement/Peer review committee
- Patient/Family (if disclosure warranted)
- Medical Executive Committee
- Radiology quality committee

### Workflow Requirements

**Immediate Actions (upon discovery):**
1. Patient clinical status assessment
2. Immediate treatment initiated if indicated
3. Physician notification (ordering provider and patient's current provider)
4. Imaging re-review and second opinion
5. Risk management notification if serious

**Within 24-48 Hours:**
1. Peer review evaluation (formal radiology peer review process)
2. Disclosure to patient/family (if significant delay/harm)
3. Chart review and clinical correlation
4. Contributing factors analysis
5. Technology assessment

**Within 1-2 Weeks:**
1. Radiology peer review committee formal review
2. Critical results communication protocol review
3. Radiologist education/feedback (protected peer review process)
4. Double-read protocols consideration for high-risk studies
5. Quality improvement action plan

### Severity Level Guidelines - Healthcare Specific

**Critical:**
- Death or major permanent loss of function unrelated to natural course of illness
- Sentinel events per Joint Commission (wrong site surgery, suicide, infant abduction, etc.)
- Never Events (wrong patient, retained foreign object, severe injury/death in restraints, etc.)
- Severe permanent harm requiring life-saving intervention or major surgery

**High:**
- Permanent harm or moderate permanent disability
- Near-miss sentinel events (caught before harm but high potential)
- Serious injury requiring major intervention or ICU admission
- Regulatory reportable events (state, CMS, FDA)
- Extended hospital stay (>7 days due to incident)

**Medium:**
- Temporary harm requiring intervention or treatment
- Moderate patient care impact with recovery expected
- Medical treatment case
- Extended observation or monitoring required
- Reportable to quality/safety committees

**Low:**
- No harm or minimal harm with minimal intervention
- Near-miss caught before reaching patient
- Process failures with no patient impact
- First aid or comfort measures only

### NCC MERP Medication Error Harm Index

- **Category A:** Circumstances with capacity to cause error (no error occurred)
- **Category B:** Error occurred but did not reach patient
- **Category C:** Error reached patient but no harm
- **Category D:** Error reached patient, monitoring required but no harm
- **Category E:** Temporary harm, intervention required
- **Category F:** Temporary harm, hospitalization/prolonged stay
- **Category G:** Permanent harm
- **Category H:** Intervention required to sustain life
- **Category I:** Death

### Workflow and Approval Requirements

**Immediate Notifications (within 1-2 hours):**
- Sentinel events (death, severe harm)
- Never events
- Patient safety emergencies (elopement, suicide attempt)
- Events requiring immediate patient intervention

**Management Approval Required:**
- All Critical severity incidents
- High severity with regulatory reporting requirements
- Incidents requiring patient/family disclosure
- Events with litigation potential
- HACs (Hospital-Acquired Conditions)

**Committee Review Required:**
- Sentinel events → RCA by multidisciplinary team
- Medication errors (Category E-I) → P&T Committee
- Falls with injury → Fall Prevention Committee
- HAIs → Infection Prevention Committee
- All peer review cases → Appropriate peer review body

### Regulatory Reporting Timelines

- **Joint Commission Sentinel Event:** Within 15 days of event or becoming aware
- **State Department of Health (serious events):** Immediate to 24 hours (varies by state)
- **CMS (Never Events/HACs):** Per claims submission timelines
- **FDA MedWatch (serious adverse events):** Within 24-48 hours
- **NHSN (HAI reporting):** Monthly reporting cycles
- **Law Enforcement (infant abduction, patient death investigation):** Immediate

### Document Retention Schedule - Healthcare

- **Sentinel Events / Never Events:** Permanent retention
- **Medical Malpractice Statute Period:** Incident reports retained for statute of limitations + 2 years (varies by state, typically 2-7 years)
- **Peer Review Documents:** Permanent (protected by state peer review statutes)
- **Quality Improvement Data:** 7-10 years minimum
- **Regulatory Reports:** Per agency requirements (typically 5-7 years minimum)
- **Patient Disclosure Documentation:** Part of medical record (per medical record retention - typically 7-10 years adults, 7+ years after age 18 for pediatrics)

### HIPAA Compliance in Incident Reporting

**Critical Requirements:**
1. **Minimum Necessary:** Only collect PHI necessary for investigation and quality improvement
2. **De-identification:** Use medical record numbers, not patient names in general incident reports
3. **Access Controls:** Role-based access to incident reports containing PHI
4. **Audit Trails:** Track all access to incident reports with patient information
5. **Encryption:** PHI must be encrypted at rest and in transit
6. **Breach Assessment:** Determine if incident itself constitutes a HIPAA breach (e.g., lost records, unauthorized access)

**Permitted Uses (without patient authorization):**
- Treatment, Payment, and Healthcare Operations (TPO)
- Quality improvement and patient safety activities
- Required regulatory reporting
- Risk management and legal compliance
- Peer review (protected by state laws)
