# MINING INDUSTRY INCIDENT MANAGEMENT USE CASES

## Document Overview
This document provides comprehensive, day-to-day incident management use cases specific to the mining and industrial sector. These use cases are based on 15+ years of mining operations experience and cover all major incident types encountered in surface and underground mining operations.

---

## USE CASE 1: UNDERGROUND EQUIPMENT FIRE

### Description
Fire or smoke detected in underground mobile equipment (loaders, haul trucks, drills) requiring immediate evacuation and emergency response.

### Frequency
Monthly to Quarterly

### Severity Range
**High to Critical**
- High: Small fire contained quickly, no injuries, minimal equipment damage
- Critical: Major fire, evacuation required, personnel endangered, significant equipment loss

### Key Data Points Required
- Equipment ID and type
- Underground location (level, drift, stope number)
- Time of detection
- Number of personnel evacuated
- Fire suppression method used
- Equipment damage assessment
- Production impact (hours lost)
- Root cause (electrical, hydraulic, fuel system)
- Fire detection system performance
- Emergency response time
- Ventilation system status during event
- Hazardous materials involved

### Regulatory Requirements
- MSHA 30 CFR Part 50 (immediate notification for serious accidents)
- State mining authority notification within 24 hours
- OSHA recordkeeping if injuries occur
- Internal fire investigation report
- Emergency preparedness plan review

### Stakeholders
- Mine Rescue Team
- Underground Supervisor
- Safety Manager
- Maintenance Manager
- Operations Manager
- Regulatory Affairs
- Insurance
- MSHA Inspector (if serious)
- Corporate Risk Management

### Workflow Requirements
1. **Immediate (0-1 hour):**
   - Emergency response activation
   - Evacuation verification
   - Fire suppression deployment

2. **Within 24 hours:**
   - Investigation initiated
   - Regulatory notifications completed
   - Preliminary report to management

3. **Within 72 hours:**
   - Root cause analysis completed
   - Equipment inspection scheduled
   - Safety bulletin distributed

4. **Approval Requirements:**
   - Executive management approval to resume operations
   - Safety Manager sign-off on corrective actions
   - Regulatory approval if required

## USE CASE 2: SLIP, TRIP, AND FALL INJURY

### Description
Worker sustains injury from slipping on wet surface, tripping over equipment/materials, or falling from elevation in surface or underground operations.

### Frequency
Weekly to Daily

### Severity Range
**Low to High**
- Low: Minor bruising, first aid only, no time lost
- Medium: Sprain/strain, restricted duty, medical treatment
- High: Fracture, laceration requiring sutures, hospitalization
- Critical: Head injury, spinal injury, multiple fractures

### Key Data Points Required
- Injured person details (name, employee ID, contractor status)
- Injury type and body part affected
- Exact location and conditions (wet, icy, debris, lighting)
- PPE being worn
- Task being performed
- Witness statements
- Photos of scene
- Days away from work / restricted duty
- First aid vs. medical treatment case
- Contributing factors (housekeeping, lighting, surface condition)
- Environmental conditions
- Previous incidents in same location

### Regulatory Requirements
- MSHA Part 50 reporting if days away from work
- OSHA 300 log entry
- Workers' compensation filing
- Potential MSHA 104(a) citation review
- Return to work documentation

### Stakeholders
- Injured worker
- Direct supervisor
- Safety department
- Medical/First aid
- HR
- Workers' compensation carrier
- Union representative (if applicable)
- MSHA (if recordable)

### Workflow Requirements
1. **Immediate:**
   - First aid/medical assessment
   - Scene preservation and photos
   - Initial notification to supervisor

2. **Within 24 hours:**
   - Supervisor investigation completed
   - Workers' comp filing if required
   - Corrective actions identified

3. **Within 48 hours:**
   - Safety department review and approval
   - Root cause determination
   - Similar hazards identified site-wide

4. **Ongoing:**
   - Return to work clearance from medical
   - Corrective action tracking and verification

## USE CASE 3: HAUL TRUCK COLLISION OR ROLLOVER

### Description
Surface haul truck involved in collision with other equipment, structures, or rollover on haul road.

### Frequency
Monthly

### Severity Range
**Medium to Critical**
- Medium: Minor collision, vehicle damage only, no injuries
- High: Significant collision, injuries sustained, major vehicle damage
- Critical: Rollover with serious injury or fatality

### Key Data Points Required
- Vehicle IDs involved
- Operator names and experience levels
- Location (GPS coordinates, haul road segment)
- Speed at time of incident (from monitoring system)
- Road conditions (wet, icy, grade percentage)
- Visibility conditions
- Fatigue monitoring data
- Pre-shift inspection records
- Damage assessment ($ value)
- Load being carried
- Injuries sustained
- Telematics data download
- Dispatch communications
- Road maintenance history

### Regulatory Requirements
- MSHA serious accident reporting if fatality/serious injury
- DOT reporting if vehicle weighs over 10,000 lbs
- State transportation authority if on public road
- Drug and alcohol testing per MSHA/company policy
- Vehicle inspection records review

### Stakeholders
- Vehicle operators
- Dispatch
- Maintenance
- Safety Manager
- Operations Manager
- Fleet Manager
- Insurance
- Legal (if serious)
- MSHA Inspector (for serious accidents)
- Drug/Alcohol testing coordinator

### Workflow Requirements
1. **Immediate:**
   - Operator drug/alcohol test if applicable
   - Scene secured and investigated
   - Medical response if injuries

2. **Within 4 hours:**
   - Download telematics/monitoring data
   - Witness interviews
   - Preliminary report

3. **Within 48 hours:**
   - Root cause analysis
   - Safety alert issued to all operators
   - Operator retraining or discipline review

4. **Approval:**
   - Management review and approval
   - Return to service clearance for vehicles
   - Regulatory reporting completion

## USE CASE 4: CHEMICAL SPILL OR RELEASE

### Description
Unintended release of hazardous chemicals including diesel fuel, hydraulic fluid, explosives materials, reagents (cyanide, sulfuric acid), or other regulated substances.

### Frequency
Weekly to Monthly

### Severity Range
**Low to Critical**
- Low: Minor spill (<5 gallons), contained immediately, no environmental impact
- Medium: Moderate spill (5-50 gallons), contained on-site, minor cleanup
- High: Large spill (>50 gallons), environmental impact potential
- Critical: Reportable quantity release, water contamination, personnel exposure

### Key Data Points Required
- Chemical name and SDS reference
- Quantity spilled (gallons/liters)
- Location (coordinates if environmental impact)
- Containment measures taken
- Environmental impact (soil, water, air)
- Cause of release (equipment failure, human error, overfill)
- Cleanup method and contractor used
- Waste disposal tracking numbers
- Personnel exposure
- Weather conditions
- Proximity to water bodies
- Soil samples collected
- Cleanup completion documentation

### Regulatory Requirements
- EPA reportable quantity (RQ) notification
- State environmental agency notification
- MSHA/OSHA if worker exposure
- National Response Center (if reportable quantity)
- Community right-to-know notifications
- Hazardous waste manifests
- Environmental restoration plan

### Stakeholders
- Environmental Manager
- Safety Manager
- Operations
- Maintenance
- Spill response team/contractor
- Regulatory Affairs
- EPA/State inspectors
- Legal
- Public Relations (if community impact)
- Emergency response coordinator

### Workflow Requirements
1. **Immediate (0-2 hours):**
   - Containment and notification
   - Regulatory notification within required timeframes
   - Environmental assessment

2. **Within 24 hours:**
   - Third-party cleanup mobilized if major spill
   - Soil/water sampling initiated
   - Preliminary report submitted

3. **Within 72 hours:**
   - Corrective action plan developed
   - Root cause analysis
   - Equipment inspection/repair

4. **Long-term:**
   - Regulatory report submission
   - Management approval of final report
   - Monitoring and verification

## USE CASE 5: NEAR MISS - FALLING OBJECT/GROUND FALL

### Description
Rock, material, or equipment falls from height or roof/wall but does not strike personnel or equipment; includes scaling incidents and rib/highwall sloughing.

### Frequency
Daily to Weekly

### Severity Range
**Low to Medium** (potential for high)
- Low: Small rock fall, no proximity to personnel
- Medium: Significant material fall, personnel in adjacent area
- High potential: Large ground fall near active work area

### Key Data Points Required
- Location (specific stope, bench, level)
- Size and type of material
- Distance fallen
- Proximity to personnel/equipment (feet/meters)
- Ground conditions
- Last ground inspection date
- Scaling/support in area
- Geotechnical conditions
- Photos
- Weather conditions (freeze/thaw cycles)
- Blast impacts in area
- Seismic activity

### Regulatory Requirements
- Internal tracking only (unless injury occurs)
- Geotechnical review may be required
- Ground control plan review
- MSHA ground control standards compliance

### Stakeholders
- Area supervisor
- Ground control engineer
- Geotechnical department
- Safety department
- Miners working in area
- Mine engineer

### Workflow Requirements
1. **Immediate:**
   - Area barricade
   - Personnel removed from area
   - Initial assessment

2. **Within 4 hours:**
   - Geotechnical assessment before re-entry
   - Ground support evaluation
   - Photos and documentation

3. **Within 24 hours:**
   - Supervisor investigation
   - Trending analysis for patterns
   - Safety meeting discussion

4. **Approval:**
   - Ground control approval to resume work
   - Corrective measures implemented

## USE CASE 6: CONTRACTOR INJURY OR INCIDENT

### Description
Third-party contractor employee involved in incident on mine property during construction, maintenance, or service work.

### Frequency
Weekly to Monthly

### Severity Range
**Low to Critical**
- Follows same severity criteria as employee incidents
- Additional complexity due to contractor relationship

### Key Data Points Required
- Contractor company name
- Worker details
- Contractor's insurance information
- Task being performed under contract
- Mine supervision present
- Contractor training records
- JSA/permit for work
- All standard injury data points
- Contractor's incident report
- Contractor safety program compliance
- Work authorization documentation
- Contractor safety orientation records

### Regulatory Requirements
- MSHA reporting under mine operator's ID
- Contractor's own OSHA/MSHA obligations
- Mine operator's recordability determination
- Insurance carrier notifications (both parties)
- Contractual notification requirements

### Stakeholders
- Contractor supervisor
- Mine supervisor
- Contractor management
- Safety Manager
- Procurement/Contracts
- Legal
- Both insurance carriers
- MSHA
- Contract administrator

### Workflow Requirements
1. **Immediate:**
   - Same emergency response as employee
   - Notification to both organizations
   - Joint investigation initiation

2. **Within 24 hours:**
   - Contractor's incident report required
   - Mine's independent investigation
   - Regulatory reporting determination

3. **Within 72 hours:**
   - Joint root cause analysis
   - Contractor performance review
   - Determination if contract continues

4. **Approval:**
   - Both parties' management approval
   - Lessons learned shared
   - Pre-qualification review if serious

## USE CASE 7: EXPLOSIVE MISFIRE OR BLAST INCIDENT

### Description
Explosive charge fails to detonate, partial detonation occurs, or unexpected blast results occur including flyrock or ground vibration complaints.

### Frequency
Monthly to Quarterly

### Severity Range
**Medium to Critical**
- Medium: Single misfire, safely resolved
- High: Multiple misfires, flyrock beyond blast area
- Critical: Personnel injury, property damage, community impact

### Key Data Points Required
- Blast pattern location
- Number of holes/charges
- Type and quantity of explosives
- Blaster ID and certification
- Weather conditions
- Misfired hole locations
- Re-entry protocol followed
- Flyrock distance and damage
- Seismograph readings
- Nearby structures/residents affected
- Blast design review
- Detonation timing sequence
- Witness accounts

### Regulatory Requirements
- MSHA 30 CFR Part 56/57 blasting regulations
- ATF notification if required
- State blasting permits and regulations
- Community notifications per permit
- Blaster incident report
- Misfire resolution documentation

### Stakeholders
- Certified blaster
- Blast crew
- Operations management
- Safety Manager
- Environmental (if complaints)
- ATF (if serious)
- MSHA
- Explosive supplier
- Community relations
- Legal (if property damage)

### Workflow Requirements
1. **Immediate:**
   - Area evacuation and control
   - Re-entry waiting period compliance (8-24 hours)
   - Regulatory notifications

2. **Within 24 hours:**
   - Misfire resolution by qualified personnel
   - Investigation of cause
   - Blast plan review

3. **Within 48 hours:**
   - Seismograph data review
   - Community complaint response
   - Blaster retraining if required

4. **Approval:**
   - Management approval to resume blasting
   - Regulatory sign-off if required

## USE CASE 8: MOBILE EQUIPMENT BRAKE FAILURE

### Description
Hydraulic, pneumatic, or service/parking brake system fails on mobile equipment creating runaway or control hazard.

### Frequency
Monthly

### Severity Range
**Medium to High**
- Medium: Parking brake failure while parked, no movement
- High: Service brake degradation during operation, near miss
- Critical: Complete brake failure, collision or runaway

### Key Data Points Required
- Equipment ID and type
- Location when failure occurred
- Brake system affected (service, parking, emergency)
- Warning signs prior to failure
- Last maintenance date
- Pre-shift inspection checklist
- Operator actions taken
- Equipment age and hours
- Near miss or collision result
- Maintenance history review
- Brake fluid levels/condition
- Component failure analysis

### Regulatory Requirements
- MSHA equipment safety standards 30 CFR 56/57.14000 series
- Equipment defect tracking
- Pre-shift examination compliance
- Maintenance records retention

### Stakeholders
- Equipment operator
- Maintenance supervisor
- Maintenance technician
- Parts/inventory
- Operations supervisor
- Safety department
- Equipment manufacturer (if warranty/recall)
- Fleet manager

### Workflow Requirements
1. **Immediate:**
   - Equipment lockout/tagout
   - Area secured
   - Operator statement

2. **Within 24 hours:**
   - Maintenance investigation
   - Review of inspection records
   - Parts failure analysis

3. **Within 48 hours:**
   - Fleet-wide inspection if systemic issue
   - Root cause determination
   - Safety alert if needed

4. **Approval:**
   - Maintenance sign-off for return to service
   - Operations approval
   - Testing verification

## USE CASE 9: CONFINED SPACE EMERGENCY

### Description
Emergency situation in confined space including oxygen deficiency, toxic atmosphere, engulfment, entrapment, or injury to worker in permitted confined space.

### Frequency
Quarterly to Rare

### Severity Range
**High to Critical**
- High: Emergency requiring rescue, no serious injury
- Critical: Atmospheric emergency, serious injury, or fatality

### Key Data Points Required
- Confined space location and ID
- Entry permit details
- Atmospheric testing results (O2, LEL, H2S, CO)
- Number of entrants
- Standby personnel
- Rescue method used
- Time in space before emergency
- Type of emergency (atmospheric, injury, entrapment)
- Rescue time
- Injuries sustained
- Equipment involved
- Ventilation used
- Communication system performance

### Regulatory Requirements
- MSHA/OSHA confined space regulations
- Serious accident reporting
- Rescue team requirements
- Permit compliance verification
- Atmospheric monitoring requirements

### Stakeholders
- Entrant(s)
- Attendant
- Entry supervisor
- Rescue team
- Safety Manager
- Operations Manager
- Medical
- MSHA (if serious)
- Mine rescue consultant

### Workflow Requirements
1. **Immediate:**
   - Emergency rescue activation
   - Non-entry rescue attempted first
   - Emergency services notification

2. **Within 24 hours:**
   - Permit review and investigation
   - Atmospheric data review
   - Rescue team performance evaluation

3. **Within 72 hours:**
   - Confined space program review
   - Root cause analysis
   - Retraining of entry team

4. **Approval:**
   - Program modifications if needed
   - Executive review for serious incidents
   - Return to service approval

## USE CASE 10: ELECTRICAL SHOCK OR ARC FLASH

### Description
Worker contacts energized electrical equipment, experiences shock, or is exposed to arc flash event from electrical fault.

### Frequency
Quarterly to Rare

### Severity Range
**High to Critical**
- High: Shock with medical treatment, no permanent injury
- Critical: Severe burns, cardiac event, permanent injury, death

### Key Data Points Required
- Voltage level
- Equipment involved
- Task being performed
- LOTO procedure compliance
- Arc flash PPE worn
- Electrical worker qualifications
- Burn severity and type
- Arc flash boundary calculations
- Equipment damage
- Witness accounts
- Lockout/tagout documentation
- Arc flash labels present
- Electrical safety program compliance

### Regulatory Requirements
- MSHA electrical safety standards
- OSHA recordability (almost always)
- NFPA 70E compliance review
- Serious injury reporting
- Qualified person verification
- Electrical safety program audit

### Stakeholders
- Injured worker
- Electrical supervisor
- Electrical department
- Safety Manager
- Maintenance Manager
- Medical
- Engineering
- MSHA
- Workers' comp
- Electrical contractor (if involved)

### Workflow Requirements
1. **Immediate:**
   - Medical response
   - Equipment lockout and investigation
   - Scene preservation

2. **Within 24 hours:**
   - Electrical system review
   - Arc flash study verification
   - Qualified person training verification

3. **Within 72 hours:**
   - Root cause analysis
   - Electrical safety program review
   - PPE requirements review

4. **Approval:**
   - Return to work medical clearance
   - Senior management investigation review
   - System modifications approved

```typescript
  requiresApproval: true,
  defaultSeverity: 'high',
  requiredFields: ['vehicle_id', 'operator', 'gps_location'],
  customFields: [...]
}
// ... additional categories
```

### Common Custom Fields for Mining

1. **Equipment Identification:**
   - Equipment ID/Number
   - Equipment Type
   - Equipment Age/Hours
   - Last Maintenance Date

2. **Location Data:**
   - GPS Coordinates
   - Underground Level/Drift/Stope
   - Surface Pit/Bench Location
   - Department/Work Area

3. **Personnel Information:**
   - Employee ID/Contractor
   - Experience Level
   - Training/Certification Status
   - Shift/Crew Assignment

4. **Environmental Conditions:**
   - Weather Conditions
   - Ground Conditions
   - Visibility
   - Temperature

5. **Regulatory Data:**
   - MSHA Reportable (Yes/No)
   - Days Away from Work
   - Restricted Duty Days
   - Medical Treatment Case

### Severity Level Guidelines

**Critical:**
- Fatality or permanent total disability
- Serious injury requiring hospitalization >24 hours
- Major equipment fire or explosion
- Significant environmental release (reportable quantity)
- Events requiring MSHA immediate notification

**High:**
- Lost time injury (days away from work)
- Major equipment damage (>$100K)
- Ground control failures
- Serious near-miss with high potential
- Multiple personnel affected

**Medium:**
- Medical treatment case (no lost time)
- Equipment damage ($10K-$100K)
- Minor environmental release (contained)
- Process safety incidents
- Recordable near-miss

**Low:**
- First aid only
- Minor equipment damage (<$10K)
- Property damage only
- Process deviations
- Good catches/near-miss reporting

### Workflow and Approval Requirements

**Immediate Notifications (within 1 hour):**
- Critical incidents
- Fatalities
- Serious injuries
- Major fires/explosions
- Significant environmental releases

**Management Approval Required:**
- All Critical severity incidents
- High severity with potential regulatory impact
- Incidents requiring public disclosure
- Contractor serious injuries
- Equipment out of service >48 hours

**Regulatory Reporting Timelines:**
- MSHA Serious Accident: Immediate (within 15 minutes)
- MSHA Reportable Injury: Within 8 hours
- EPA Reportable Release: Immediate to 24 hours (varies)
- OSHA Severe Injury: Within 24 hours
- Workers' Comp: Within 24-48 hours (varies by state)

### Document Retention Schedule

- **Fatality/Serious Injury:** Permanent retention
- **Lost Time Injuries:** 30+ years (MSHA requirements)
- **Medical Treatment:** 5-7 years minimum
- **Near Miss Reports:** 3-5 years
- **Environmental Incidents:** 7+ years (EPA requirements)
- **Equipment Incidents:** Life of equipment + 5 years
- **Contractor Incidents:** Duration of contract + 7 years

## TECHNICAL INTEGRATION NOTES

### System Requirements

1. **Photo/Video Documentation:** All use cases require ability to attach media files
2. **GPS Integration:** Location tracking for surface incidents
3. **Equipment Integration:** Link to maintenance management system
4. **Regulatory Reporting:** Automated form generation for MSHA, OSHA, EPA
5. **Mobile Access:** Field reporting capability for remote mine sites
6. **Offline Capability:** Incident entry when network unavailable
7. **Notification System:** Escalation rules based on severity and type
8. **Analytics Dashboard:** Trending, leading/lagging indicators
9. **Document Management:** Investigation reports, photos, regulatory submissions
10. **Audit Trail:** Complete change tracking for regulatory compliance

### Data Privacy Considerations

- Employee names/IDs: Restricted access based on role
- Medical information: HIPAA-level protection even though not healthcare
- Investigation findings: Legal privilege considerations
- Witness statements: Confidentiality protections
- Drug/alcohol test results: Highly restricted access
