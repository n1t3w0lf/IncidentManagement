## DETAILED USE CASES

### 1. CUSTOMER SLIP, TRIP & FALL INCIDENTS

**Use Case Name:** Customer Slip/Trip/Fall Incident

**Description:**
A customer experiences a fall due to wet floors, uneven surfaces, merchandise in aisles, or other hazards. This is one of the most common and legally significant incidents in retail operations. Proper documentation is critical for liability protection and insurance claims.

**Frequency:** Daily to Weekly (varies by store size and traffic)

**Severity Range:** Low to Critical
- Low: Minor stumble, no injury, customer declines assistance
- Medium: Fall with minor injury, requires first aid
- High: Fall with significant injury, requires medical transport
- Critical: Severe injury, loss of consciousness, head trauma

**Key Data Points:**
- Date, time, and exact location of incident (aisle number, department, GPS coordinates if parking lot)
- Customer information (name, contact, witness permission)
- Detailed description of how fall occurred
- Environmental conditions (wet floor, spill, weather conditions)
- Floor surface type and condition
- Warning signs present (yes/no, type, location)
- Lighting conditions
- Footwear type customer was wearing
- Injuries claimed/observed (body parts affected)
- Medical assistance provided (first aid, EMS called, hospital transport)
- Witness statements (employees and other customers)
- Photos of scene, hazard, injuries (if permitted)
- Manager on duty information
- Incident number from security/surveillance system
- Camera coverage confirmation
- Maintenance records for area (last cleaning, inspection)

**Regulatory Requirements:**
- OSHA recordkeeping requirements (if employee slip/fall)
- State-specific premises liability reporting
- Workers' compensation notification (if employee)
- Insurance carrier notification (typically within 24-48 hours)
- Store chain corporate risk management notification
- Document retention requirements (typically 7-10 years for liability claims)

**Stakeholders:**
- Store Manager
- Department Manager
- Loss Prevention Manager
- Risk Management/Corporate Safety
- Insurance Claims Department
- Legal/General Counsel (for significant injuries)
- Facilities/Maintenance Manager
- District/Regional Manager (for severe incidents)
- Corporate Communications (for media-worthy incidents)

**Workflow Requirements:**
1. Immediate incident report creation by manager on duty
2. Scene preservation and photography (within 15 minutes)
3. Witness statement collection (within 1 hour)
4. Store manager review and approval (within 4 hours)
5. Loss prevention review (within 24 hours)
6. Risk management notification (within 24 hours for injuries requiring medical attention)
7. Insurance claim initiation (within 48 hours)
8. Follow-up investigation for pattern analysis (within 7 days)
9. Corrective action implementation and verification
10. Monthly trending analysis for prevention strategies

### 2. SHOPLIFTING & THEFT INCIDENTS

**Use Case Name:** Shoplifting/Theft/Loss Prevention Incident

**Description:**
Detection of merchandise theft by customers, including observed shoplifting, detention of suspects, recovery of merchandise, and organized retail crime activities. Includes both apprehended and non-apprehended theft incidents.

**Frequency:** Daily to Multiple Times Daily (high-volume stores)

**Severity Range:** Low to High
- Low: Suspected theft, no apprehension, low-value merchandise (<$50)
- Medium: Apprehension made, merchandise value $50-$500
- High: Organized retail crime, violent shoplifter, merchandise value >$500, law enforcement involvement

**Key Data Points:**
- Date, time, and location of observed theft
- Subject description (physical characteristics, clothing, identifying marks)
- Suspect information (if apprehended: name, ID, contact information)
- Merchandise involved (SKUs, quantities, total retail value)
- Method of theft (concealment, bag switching, receipt fraud, etc.)
- Surveillance camera coverage (camera numbers, time stamps)
- Employee witness information
- Apprehension details (location, time, circumstances)
- Law enforcement involvement (officer name, badge number, case number)
- Subject's statements
- Resistance or violence indicators
- Recovery information (merchandise condition, resalable/damaged)
- Trespass notice issued (yes/no)
- Civil demand applicable (yes/no, amount)
- Criminal charges filed (yes/no, charges)
- Prior incidents with same subject
- Evidence collected (photos, video timestamps, physical evidence)
- Loss Prevention Officer details
- Store prosecution policy followed (yes/no)

**Regulatory Requirements:**
- State shoplifting/merchant detention laws compliance
- Merchant privilege statute requirements
- Civil recovery/demand letter regulations (varies by state)
- Criminal trespass notice requirements
- Juvenile reporting requirements (if minor involved)
- Law enforcement reporting thresholds (varies by jurisdiction)
- Organized retail crime reporting (to state task forces)
- Record retention for criminal proceedings (varies by state, typically 3-7 years)

**Stakeholders:**
- Loss Prevention Officer/Manager
- Store Manager
- Department Manager (where theft occurred)
- District Loss Prevention Manager
- Regional/Corporate Loss Prevention
- Legal Department
- Law Enforcement
- Corporate Security Operations Center
- Prosecution Support Team
- Civil Recovery Department
- Merchandise Planning (for inventory adjustment)

**Workflow Requirements:**
1. Incident documentation during or immediately after observation
2. Surveillance video preservation and tagging (immediate)
3. Merchandise recovery and documentation
4. Store manager notification (immediate for apprehensions)
5. Law enforcement contact (as per company policy and state law)
6. Subject processing and documentation
7. Loss Prevention Manager review (within 24 hours)
8. Video evidence package creation (within 48 hours if prosecution)
9. Civil demand letter generation (within 30 days if applicable)
10. District LP review for pattern analysis (weekly)
11. Organized retail crime reporting (if applicable, within 24 hours)
12. Case closure and disposition tracking

### 3. EMPLOYEE WORKPLACE INJURIES

**Use Case Name:** Employee Injury/Workers' Compensation Incident

**Description:**
Any injury sustained by an employee during work hours or on company premises, including cuts, burns, strains, falls, repetitive motion injuries, and equipment-related injuries. Critical for workers' compensation compliance and OSHA recordkeeping.

**Frequency:** Weekly to Monthly (varies by store size and department)

**Severity Range:** Low to Critical
- Low: Minor cut, bruise, or strain; first aid treatment only
- Medium: Injury requiring medical treatment beyond first aid
- High: Lost workday injury, restricted duty required
- Critical: Hospitalization, amputation, fatality, catastrophic injury

**Key Data Points:**
- Employee information (name, ID, job title, department, hire date)
- Date and time of injury
- Exact location of incident
- Detailed description of how injury occurred
- Body part(s) injured
- Type of injury (laceration, contusion, sprain, burn, etc.)
- Equipment or tools involved
- Task being performed at time of injury
- Supervisor present (yes/no)
- Witnesses (employee names and statements)
- Immediate treatment provided
- Medical care required (first aid, clinic, ER, hospitalization)
- Healthcare provider information
- Time employee stopped working
- Return to work status
- Work restrictions/modified duty
- PPE being used (yes/no, type)
- Safety training completion date
- Environmental conditions
- Photos of scene and equipment
- Root cause analysis
- Corrective actions required
- Days away from work
- OSHA recordability determination

**Regulatory Requirements:**
- OSHA 300 Log recordkeeping (injuries requiring medical treatment beyond first aid)
- OSHA 301 Incident Report (within 7 days)
- OSHA Form 300A annual summary posting (Feb 1 - Apr 30)
- State workers' compensation reporting (typically 24-48 hours)
- Workers' compensation first report of injury filing
- Severe injury reporting to OSHA (hospitalizations within 24 hours, fatalities within 8 hours)
- State-specific injury reporting requirements
- Medical privacy/HIPAA compliance
- ADA/reasonable accommodation considerations
- Record retention (OSHA records: 5 years; workers' comp: varies by state)

**Stakeholders:**
- Injured Employee
- Direct Supervisor
- Store Manager
- Human Resources Manager
- Corporate Safety/Risk Management
- Workers' Compensation Administrator
- OSHA Coordinator
- Healthcare Provider/Clinic
- Workers' Compensation Insurance Carrier
- District/Regional Manager
- Legal Department (for severe injuries)
- Return to Work Coordinator

**Workflow Requirements:**
1. Immediate supervisor notification and incident documentation
2. Medical assessment and treatment authorization (immediate)
3. Store manager review and approval (within 2 hours)
4. Workers' compensation report filing (within 24 hours)
5. HR notification (within 24 hours)
6. OSHA recordability determination (within 7 days)
7. Root cause investigation (within 3 days)
8. Corrective action plan development (within 7 days)
9. Follow-up medical documentation tracking
10. Return to work coordination
11. Modified duty assignment tracking
12. Closure upon full return to work or case settlement
13. Quarterly injury trend analysis
14. Annual OSHA 300A preparation and posting

### 4. CASH HANDLING DISCREPANCIES

**Use Case Name:** Cash Over/Short and Till Discrepancy

**Description:**
Variances discovered during cash register reconciliation, including overages, shortages, missing deposits, and suspicious cash handling patterns. Critical for fraud prevention and financial controls.

**Frequency:** Daily to Weekly

**Severity Range:** Low to High
- Low: Minor variance <$5, isolated occurrence
- Medium: Variance $5-$50, or pattern of small shortages
- High: Variance >$50, suspected theft, pattern indicating fraud

**Key Data Points:**
- Date and time of discovery
- Register/till number
- Cashier name and employee ID
- Shift worked (start/end times)
- Amount of variance (over/short)
- Expected cash amount
- Actual cash amount counted
- Transaction count for shift
- Last balanced amount
- Supervisor who performed count
- Secondary count performed (yes/no, by whom)
- Currency denominations breakdown
- Non-cash tenders (checks, gift cards, credit cards)
- Void transactions during shift
- Refund transactions during shift
- Manager override count
- Suspicious transaction patterns
- Previous variance history for cashier
- Surveillance coverage confirmation
- Video review conducted (yes/no, findings)
- Deposit slip discrepancies
- Safe count discrepancies
- Cashier statement/explanation
- Disciplinary action taken (if applicable)

**Regulatory Requirements:**
- Internal audit compliance
- Financial controls policy compliance
- Bank Secrecy Act considerations (for large discrepancies)
- State labor law compliance (regarding till shortage deductions)
- Document retention for financial audits (typically 7 years)
- Corporate accounting notification thresholds
- Loss prevention investigation protocols

**Stakeholders:**
- Cashier/Employee
- Front End Supervisor/Manager
- Store Manager
- Accounting Department
- Loss Prevention Manager
- District Manager (for significant variances)
- Internal Audit
- HR Manager (if disciplinary action required)
- Payroll (if applicable to employee accountability)

**Workflow Requirements:**
1. Immediate documentation upon discovery
2. Secondary count verification by manager
3. Cashier notification and statement collection
4. Store manager review (same day)
5. Pattern analysis review (for repeat occurrences)
6. Video surveillance review (within 24 hours for variances >$20)
7. Loss prevention investigation (for variances >$50 or patterns)
8. Disciplinary action determination (within 3 days)
9. Accounting department notification (daily for all variances)
10. Monthly trending analysis by cashier and location
11. Retraining or corrective action implementation
12. Case closure upon resolution or completion of investigation

### 5. PRODUCT DAMAGE & QUALITY ISSUES

**Use Case Name:** Product Damage, Defect, and Quality Complaint

**Description:**
Discovery of damaged merchandise, defective products, customer complaints about product quality or safety, product recalls, and vendor quality issues. Includes both pre-sale damage and post-purchase complaints.

**Frequency:** Daily to Weekly

**Severity Range:** Low to High
- Low: Minor cosmetic damage, isolated defect, non-safety issue
- Medium: Functional defect, multiple units affected, customer dissatisfaction
- High: Safety hazard, product recall, potential injury, vendor pattern

**Key Data Points:**
- Date and time of discovery/complaint
- Product information (SKU, UPC, description, brand)
- Lot number or serial number
- Quantity affected
- Location found (stockroom, shelf, customer home)
- Type of damage/defect (describe in detail)
- Safety hazard present (yes/no, describe)
- Customer complaint details (if applicable)
- Customer information (name, contact, receipt number)
- Purchase date and location
- Photos of damage/defect
- Vendor/supplier information
- Receiving date for affected batch
- Other units inspected (quantity, findings)
- Product removed from floor (yes/no)
- Product quarantined (yes/no, location)
- Vendor notification (yes/no, date)
- Customer resolution (refund, exchange, store credit)
- Recall check performed (yes/no)
- Similar complaints history
- Root cause (shipping damage, manufacturing defect, storage issue, etc.)
- Corrective action required
- Regulatory agency notification needed (FDA, CPSC, etc.)

**Regulatory Requirements:**
- FDA reporting (for food safety issues)
- CPSC reporting (for consumer product safety hazards)
- State health department notification (for food contamination)
- Product recall compliance (FDA, CPSC, or manufacturer recall)
- Documentation retention for liability claims (7-10 years)
- Vendor contract compliance
- Quality assurance protocols

**Stakeholders:**
- Customer (if complaint)
- Sales Associate/Department Manager
- Store Manager
- Quality Assurance Manager
- Vendor/Supplier
- Purchasing/Merchandising Department
- Corporate Quality Control
- Legal Department (for safety hazards)
- Customer Service Manager
- Receiving Manager
- Regulatory Compliance Officer (if applicable)

**Workflow Requirements:**
1. Immediate product removal from sales floor (if safety concern)
2. Incident documentation with photos
3. Quarantine of affected products
4. Inspection of related inventory (same batch/lot)
5. Customer resolution (same day if complaint)
6. Store manager review (within 24 hours)
7. Vendor notification (within 48 hours)
8. Corporate quality assurance notification (within 24 hours for safety issues)
9. Regulatory notification (if required, per agency timelines)
10. Root cause investigation (within 7 days)
11. Corrective action implementation
12. Follow-up verification
13. Pattern analysis for vendor performance
14. Case closure upon resolution

### 6. CUSTOMER COMPLAINTS & SERVICE DISPUTES

**Use Case Name:** Customer Service Complaint and Dispute Resolution

**Description:**
Customer dissatisfaction with service quality, employee behavior, pricing disputes, return policy issues, and general complaints requiring management resolution. Critical for customer retention and service improvement.

**Frequency:** Daily to Multiple Times Daily

**Severity Range:** Low to High
- Low: Minor service issue, easily resolved
- Medium: Pricing dispute, return issue, employee behavior concern
- High: Threat of legal action, social media escalation, severe service failure, discrimination complaint

**Key Data Points:**
- Date and time of incident
- Customer information (name, contact, loyalty program member ID)
- Transaction details (receipt number, date, amount)
- Nature of complaint (category)
- Detailed description of issue
- Employee(s) involved (names, IDs)
- Department/location where incident occurred
- Customer's desired resolution
- Manager who handled complaint
- Resolution provided (refund, exchange, store credit, apology)
- Customer satisfaction with resolution (yes/no)
- Root cause of complaint
- Policy violation identified (yes/no, which policy)
- Coaching/training needed for employee
- Escalation level (front-line, manager, district, corporate)
- Social media mention (yes/no, platform, post details)
- Legal threat made (yes/no)
- Discrimination/harassment allegation (yes/no)
- Witness information
- Surveillance footage relevant (yes/no)
- Follow-up required (yes/no)
- Customer follow-up contact made (yes/no, date)

**Regulatory Requirements:**
- ADA compliance (if accessibility complaint)
- Civil rights/discrimination law compliance
- State consumer protection laws
- Return policy compliance with state laws
- Documentation retention for potential legal claims (3-7 years)
- Corporate complaint tracking requirements
- Social media response policies

**Stakeholders:**
- Customer
- Employee(s) involved
- Department Manager
- Store Manager
- Customer Service Manager
- District Manager (for escalations)
- Corporate Customer Relations
- Human Resources (if employee conduct issue)
- Legal Department (if legal threats or discrimination claims)
- Marketing/Social Media Team (if public complaint)
- Training Department (if service failure)

**Workflow Requirements:**
1. Immediate documentation of complaint
2. Attempt at front-line resolution
3. Manager escalation (if not resolved)
4. Customer resolution within same day (if possible)
5. Store manager review (within 24 hours)
6. Employee coaching/counseling (within 48 hours if needed)
7. Corporate notification (for severe complaints or legal threats, within 24 hours)
8. Follow-up customer contact (within 3 days)
9. Pattern analysis for repeat issues (weekly/monthly)
10. Service improvement action plan (if systemic issue)
11. Case closure upon customer satisfaction or final resolution
12. Quarterly complaint trending and analysis

### 7. PROPERTY DAMAGE & VANDALISM

**Use Case Name:** Property Damage, Vandalism, and Facility Damage

**Description:**
Intentional or accidental damage to store property, fixtures, equipment, or building structure, including vandalism, graffiti, broken windows, damaged displays, and vehicle damage to building.

**Severity Range:** Low to High
- Low: Minor cosmetic damage, easily repaired, <$500
- Medium: Functional damage requiring repair, $500-$5,000
- High: Structural damage, security breach, safety hazard, >$5,000

**Key Data Points:**
- Date and time discovered (and estimated time of occurrence)
- Location of damage (specific area, department)
- Type of damage (describe in detail)
- Extent of damage (measurements, affected area)
- Estimated repair cost
- Photos of damage (multiple angles)
- Intentional vs. accidental determination
- Suspect information (if known)
- Witness information
- Surveillance coverage (yes/no, camera numbers, timestamps)
- Video review findings
- Customer or employee involvement (if applicable)
- Environmental factors (weather, vandalism, accident)
- Safety hazard created (yes/no, describe)
- Immediate corrective action taken (barricades, warning signs, temporary repair)
- Business interruption (yes/no, extent)
- Areas closed to public (yes/no, which areas)
- Law enforcement notified (yes/no, case number)
- Insurance claim required (yes/no)
- Contractor needed for repair (yes/no, company name)
- Repair timeline
- Preventative measures needed
- Manager on duty information

**Regulatory Requirements:**
- Building code compliance for repairs
- OSHA requirements if creates workplace hazard
- Fire code compliance (if affects fire safety systems)
- ADA compliance (if affects accessibility)
- Insurance claim filing requirements
- Law enforcement reporting (for vandalism/criminal damage)
- Environmental regulations (if hazardous materials involved)
- Document retention for insurance/legal purposes (7-10 years)

**Stakeholders:**
- Store Manager
- Facilities/Maintenance Manager
- Loss Prevention Manager
- District Manager
- Corporate Facilities/Real Estate
- Insurance/Risk Management
- Contractor/Repair Vendor
- Law Enforcement (if criminal)
- Security Company (if security system affected)
- Legal Department (for significant damage)
- Operations Manager

**Workflow Requirements:**
1. Immediate area securing and safety measures
2. Incident documentation with photos
3. Store manager notification (immediate)
4. Surveillance review (within 4 hours)
5. Law enforcement notification (immediate if criminal vandalism)
6. Facilities notification (within 24 hours)
7. Insurance notification (within 24-48 hours if claim required)
8. Repair estimate and vendor engagement (within 3 days)
9. Repair completion and verification
10. Preventative measures implementation
11. District manager review (for damage >$1,000)
12. Case closure upon repair completion and payment

Issues with security cameras, alarm systems, access control failures, surveillance detection of suspicious activity, and security system malfunctions affecting store protection capabilities.

- Type of security issue (camera, alarm, access control, etc.)
- Specific equipment affected (camera numbers, alarm zones, door access points)
- Description of malfunction or incident
- Duration of system failure
- Areas without coverage during failure
- Alarm activation details (zone, time, type)
- False alarm (yes/no)
- Response time
- Law enforcement response (if alarm activation)
- Vendor/service provider notified (yes/no, company name)
- Service ticket number
- Temporary security measures implemented
- Business impact (loss of LP coverage, evidence gap)
- Related incidents during outage
- Root cause of failure
- Repair/resolution timeline
- System test after repair
- Preventative maintenance needed
- Backup systems available (yes/no)
- Manager on duty information

**Regulatory Requirements:**
- Law enforcement alarm permit compliance
- False alarm fees/fines (per local ordinances)
- Data retention policies (video storage requirements)
- Privacy law compliance (camera placement, notification)
- Cybersecurity requirements (for networked systems)
- Insurance requirements for security systems
- Document retention for security incidents (varies, typically 3-7 years)

**Stakeholders:**
- Loss Prevention Manager
- Store Manager
- Security System Vendor/Provider
- District Loss Prevention Manager
- Corporate Security Operations
- IT Department (for networked systems)
- Law Enforcement (if alarm activation)
- Facilities Manager
- Insurance/Risk Management
- Alarm Monitoring Company

**Workflow Requirements:**
1. Immediate issue identification and documentation
2. Store manager notification (immediate for critical issues)
3. Temporary security measures implementation
4. Service provider notification (within 1-2 hours)
5. Loss prevention notification (immediate)
6. Service scheduling and tracking
7. Repair/resolution completion
8. System testing and verification
9. Documentation of outage impact
10. District LP review (for significant outages)
11. Preventative maintenance scheduling
12. Case closure upon successful system restoration

### 11. FIRE SAFETY & EMERGENCY EVACUATIONS

**Use Case Name:** Fire Safety Incident and Emergency Evacuation

**Description:**
Fire emergencies, smoke conditions, fire alarm activations, sprinkler discharges, fire suppression system issues, and emergency evacuations including drills and actual emergencies.

**Frequency:** Monthly to Rare (drills more frequent than actual emergencies)

**Severity Range:** Medium to Critical
- Medium: Fire alarm false activation, drill, minor smoke condition
- High: Actual fire requiring fire department response, sprinkler activation
- Critical: Major fire, injuries, significant property damage, multi-agency response

**Key Data Points:**
- Date and time of incident/alarm
- Type of event (actual fire, smoke, alarm activation, drill)
- Alarm activation cause (if known)

- Law enforcement called (yes/no, arrival time)
- Reunification verification (ID check of caregiver)
- Surveillance review (yes/no, findings)
- Suspicious persons observed (yes/no, description)
- Employee training verification (Code Adam)
- Public announcement made (yes/no)
- Store procedures followed (yes/no)
- Caregiver statements
- Subject statements (if age appropriate)
- Photos of reunification (for documentation)

**Regulatory Requirements:**
- Code Adam protocol compliance (if store participates)
- Child safety/Amber Alert awareness
- Mandatory reporting (if abuse or neglect suspected)
- Child protective services notification (if concerns)
- Law enforcement cooperation requirements
- Privacy considerations (COPPA for children)
- Vulnerable adult protection laws (for elderly/disabled)
- Document retention (lost person incidents: 3-5 years)
- Video surveillance retention (extended for investigative purposes)

**Stakeholders:**
- Lost Person (child/elderly/vulnerable adult)
- Caregiver/Parent/Family
- Store Manager
- All Store Employees (Code Adam activation)
- Loss Prevention Manager
- Corporate Security
- Law Enforcement
- Child Protective Services (if concerns)
- Adult Protective Services (if concerns)
- District Manager
- Legal Department (if escalated)
- Public Relations (if media involvement)

**Workflow Requirements:**
1. Immediate caregiver interview for description and details
2. Code Adam activation announcement (overhead or radio)
3. Employee search team deployment to assigned areas
4. Exit monitoring (employees stationed at doors)
5. Surveillance room monitoring
6. Store perimeter check
7. Law enforcement notification (per protocol, typically if not found within 10 minutes)
8. Restroom and fitting room checks
9. Vehicle parking lot search (if applicable)
10. Subject located and secured (with employee until caregiver arrives)
11. Caregiver reunification with ID verification
12. Code Adam all-clear announcement
13. Incident documentation
14. Store manager review
15. Employee debriefing
16. Surveillance review (to identify any suspicious persons)
17. Law enforcement report (if involved)
18. Corporate notification (for significant incidents)
19. Code Adam procedure review and training reinforcement
20. Case closure upon successful reunification and follow-up

## SEVERITY LEVEL GUIDELINES FOR RETAIL

### Critical Severity Indicators:
- Fatality or life-threatening injury
- Armed robbery or active violence
- Major fire or evacuation
- Widespread data breach
- Foodborne illness outbreak
- Missing child not found within 15 minutes
- OSHA-reportable catastrophic event
- Media-worthy incident with reputation impact

### High Severity Indicators:
- Serious injury requiring hospitalization
- Theft/loss over $5,000
- Product recall
- Equipment failure causing business interruption >4 hours
- Workplace violence with weapon
- Environmental hazard requiring professional cleanup
- Customer safety hazard requiring area closure

### Medium Severity Indicators:
- Injury requiring medical treatment beyond first aid
- Theft/loss $500-$5,000
- Customer complaint escalated to corporate
- Cash discrepancy >$50
- Equipment failure affecting operations
- Minor property damage $500-$5,000
- Food safety temperature violation

### Low Severity Indicators:
- Minor injury (first aid only)
- Theft/loss <$500
- Routine customer complaint
- Cash variance <$5
- Minor equipment issue quickly resolved
- Minor product quality issue
- Isolated service issue
